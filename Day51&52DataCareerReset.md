I had to read one SQL question three times just to understand what it was even asking....

It was about calculating the median, 80th, and 95th percentile of how long customers stay on a node in a neo-bank (like a digital-only bank, no physical branches, where customers are assigned to nodes the way you'd be assigned to a bank branch)

I thought it was just another query, but I got stuck and not because of syntax, I just didn't fully understand what those numbers were actually telling me.

So I slowed down and these are what I figured out:

The median was 15 days, and the 95th percentile was 28 days.

And when the 95th percentile is much higher than the median, it shows that while most customers move through the system quickly, a smaller group stays much longer.

And that difference is important.

Because that’s often where inefficiencies, bottlenecks, or unusual cases exist.

Interestingly, when I looked at the overall findings from the case study (Data Bank), the same pattern showed up

Average time on nodes was 14 days, which looked healthy, but the moment you look past the average, a different picture shows up.

That's the thing about averages, they can make a broken system look normal.

This was a case study (Data Bank), but this kind of pattern shows up in real businesses all the time. A metric looks fine at the top level, then you dig in and find out it's been masking a problem for months.

Knowing where to look, and what questions to ask, is often more valuable than the query itself.
